As December gets closer, many business owners start asking the same question: should I buy that piece of equipment before December 31 and write it off this year?

Bonus depreciation can make a year-end purchase look attractive. In 2026, the federal rules are more generous than they have been in several years: qualifying equipment can often be written off in full in the year you place it in service. That still does not mean every purchase is a good idea. A deduction is a percentage of what you spent, not a rebate for the full cost. The better question is whether the business actually needs the asset, and whether it will be ready to use by year-end.

This article is general information about 2026 federal bonus depreciation rules for calendar-year businesses. It is not tax advice for a particular business.

## 100% bonus depreciation in 2026

The One, Big, Beautiful Bill Act, signed in July 2025, restored a 100% special depreciation allowance — often called bonus depreciation — for qualified property acquired and placed in service after January 19, 2025. The IRS confirmed that rate in Notice 2026-11. For most equipment a Florence, AL, business owner would buy in the last weeks of 2026, the full cost can be written off this year.

If you entered a written binding contract on or before January 19, 2025, the older phase-down still applies. Property acquired under that earlier rule and placed in service in 2026 generally qualifies for only 20% bonus depreciation, not 100%.

Bonus depreciation is automatic unless you elect out for a class of property. It is taken before regular depreciation. It can create or increase a loss, so if 2026 is already a thin year, talk through the timing before you accelerate a large write-off.

Used property can qualify, with limits: it generally must be purchased from an unrelated party, and you cannot have used it before. For a sole proprietor, the deduction usually reduces both income tax and self-employment tax, because it comes off business profit. That can make it more powerful, dollar for dollar, than a retirement contribution. It is still not a reason to buy equipment you do not need.

## "Placed in service" is the real deadline

Buying the asset is not enough. Ordering it is not enough. The deduction belongs to the year the property is **placed in service** — when it is ready and available for its specific business use. For a calendar-year business, that date has to fall on or before December 31, 2026.

A machine that is still on a truck, sitting in a crate, or waiting on installation in January does not count for 2026, even if you paid for it in December. If a vendor is promising year-end delivery, leave enough time for the asset to actually be ready to use.

Keep the invoice, the delivery record, and a short note of when the property was first ready to use.

## What usually qualifies — and what often does not

Common bonus depreciation property includes machinery, equipment, office furniture, computers, off-the-shelf software, and qualified improvement property (certain interior improvements to a nonresidential building). Land is never depreciable, and a new building as a whole generally does not qualify.

**Vehicles need extra care.** Passenger automobiles, including many cars, crossovers, and lighter trucks, are subject to annual depreciation caps. For a passenger auto placed in service in 2026 to which bonus depreciation applies, the first-year limit is $20,300. Some heavier trucks and vans fall outside those tighter limits, but the definitions are specific. Do not assume a vehicle is fully deductible in year one.

If you use the asset for both business and personal purposes, only the business-use portion is depreciable. For bonus depreciation, business use generally has to be more than 50%.

## When the purchase helps — and when it is just spending to save tax

A write-off is not the same as getting the asset free. The tax savings are a fraction of the cost.

As an illustration only, suppose a calendar-year business in the 24% federal income tax bracket buys $40,000 of qualifying equipment and places it in service in December. 100% bonus depreciation can deduct the full $40,000 in 2026. The federal income tax savings are about $9,600, assuming the deduction reduces taxable income this year. The business still spent $40,000, or took on a loan for that amount. After the tax benefit, the economic cost is still about $30,400, before state tax and the timing of the savings.

The purchase makes more sense when:

- The business already needs the equipment, or will need it early next year, and buying now does not strain cash or credit.
- 2026 is a profitable year, and the deduction actually reduces this year's tax.
- The asset will be placed in service by December 31, not merely ordered.
- You have compared this use of cash with other year-end options, such as a retirement contribution.

It is usually a poor trade when you are buying something you do not need, stretching the business's cash, or hoping a tax deduction will turn a weak purchase into a good one. "Keep your life free from love of money, and be content with what you have, for he has said, 'I will never leave you nor forsake you'" (Hebrews 13:5, ESV). Contentment is not an argument against investing in the business. It is a check against buying just to chase a deduction.

## A short list before you sign

- Confirm the asset will be ready and available for use by December 31.
- Know your year-to-date profit. A current profit-and-loss through November is the starting point. The [year-end bookkeeping checklist](/blog/year-end-bookkeeping-checklist-business-owners-florence) can help make that number trustworthy.
- Check whether the property qualifies, and whether any vehicle or listed-property limits apply.
- If 2026 is a thin year, consider whether taking the full write-off now is the better fit, or whether electing out for that class of property makes more sense.
- Keep invoices, delivery records, and business-use notes with the books.

For a broader year-end list, see [tax prep vs. tax planning before year-end](/blog/tax-prep-vs-tax-planning-before-year-end-florence).

## A faithful decision, not a rushed one

"The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance, but everyone who is hasty comes only to poverty" (Proverbs 21:5, ESV). A year-end equipment purchase can be wise stewardship when the business needs the asset and the tax result is understood. It is haste when the only reason to buy is the calendar.
